## Identification
IMPORTANT: frosty pod rot is caused by the fungus Moniliophthora roreri. It is reported from Central and northern South America and the Caribbean, and sources describe Africa and Asia as still free of it. It is a serious quarantine concern for West Africa. A detection of 'frosty pod' on a Nigerian farm is therefore far more likely to be black pod or another problem, but it should be checked, not ignored. True frosty pod: swellings or humps on young pods, uneven yellowing or early ripening, dark brown patches on older pods, and a cream to tan powdery crust over the pod surface. Cut pods show reddish-brown seeds. Black pod usually turns the pod dark brown to black with a white fuzz in wet weather.


## Damage and symptoms
In regions where it occurs, the disease destroys most of the crop and can cause plantations to be abandoned. The fungus affects pods only, not leaves.


## Conditions that favour it
Humid conditions and pods present during rainy periods. Spores spread by wind and water, and over long distances on moved pods, tools, clothes, and vehicles. Spores can stay alive for months on clothing and tools.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Do not move, sell, or carry pods from the suspect farm. Change clothes and clean boots and tools after visiting. Where the disease is established, farmers remove and destroy infected pods before the frosty crust forms.


## Biological and low-risk controls
No farm-level biological control is recommended in Nigeria for this disease.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Do not apply fungicide on a suspicion of frosty pod rot. Treatment of a quarantine disease should be directed by the authorities. Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved.


## When to contact an extension officer
If you see cream or tan powdery growth on pods together with humped, uneven young pods, take clear photos of the pod outside and cut open, avoid moving the pods, and report right away to the extension office, CRIN, or the Nigeria Agricultural Quarantine Service. If the pods look like ordinary black pod, follow the black pod advice.
