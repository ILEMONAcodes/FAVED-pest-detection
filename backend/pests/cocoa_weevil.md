## Identification
Weevils are small beetles with a long snout. The model class 'Weevil' comes from a cocoa pest dataset that does not name the species, and I could not confirm which weevil it shows or whether it is an important cocoa pest in Nigeria. Treat any identification as provisional until an expert confirms the species.


## Damage and symptoms
Typically, chewed notches on leaf edges, feeding scars or holes on young shoots and pods, and sometimes damage to flowers. Look closely for the beetle, which may drop to the ground when disturbed.


## Conditions that favour it
Overgrown, unpruned farms with plenty of young flush and cover, and weedy undergrowth that shelters beetles.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Keep the farm clean: weed, prune, and manage shade. Inspect young shoots and pods regularly. Where only a few beetles are present, hand-collect them early in the day. Collect and destroy badly damaged pods.


## Biological and low-risk controls
Conserve natural enemies and ants on the farm. Avoid broad-spectrum sprays that kill them.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Do not spray for weevils until the species is confirmed, because the right product depends on the insect.


## When to contact an extension officer
Take a clear photo and, if possible, collect a sample beetle in a closed container and show it to an extension officer or CRIN for species identification before you act.
