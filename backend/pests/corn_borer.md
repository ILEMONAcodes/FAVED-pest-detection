## Identification
Stem borers are cream to pinkish caterpillars with a dark head that live inside the maize stem. You usually see their damage, not the caterpillar. The model class comes from a dataset that likely shows the Asian corn borer; in West Africa the main stem borers are different species, but the damage and the management are very similar. Do not confuse with fall armyworm: borers tunnel into stems, while fall armyworm feeds on leaves and in the whorl.


## Damage and symptoms
Rows of small round holes ('shot holes') across leaves that unfolded from the whorl. 'Dead heart': the central leaves of a young plant dry and die. Sawdust-like droppings (frass) at leaf joints or in the whorl. Tunnels inside the stalk, weak or broken stalks and tassels, and sometimes damaged cobs. Losses are largest when plants are attacked young.


## Conditions that favour it
Maize stalks and stubble left in the field after harvest, which shelter caterpillars through the dry season. Continuous maize growing, late planting, and wild grasses around the field that host the borers.


## Cultural and prevention practices
After harvest, remove or destroy old stalks and stubble. Plant early with the first rains and avoid staggered planting. Rotate maize with legumes or other non-cereal crops. Intercrop maize with legumes. Researchers have also developed a 'push-pull' system that uses desmodium between maize rows and napier grass around the field to reduce stem borers and striga; the extension service can advise if it suits your farm.


## Biological and low-risk controls
Parasitic wasps and predators help to keep stem borers down. Protecting field margins and avoiding unnecessary spraying lets these helpers work. Intercropping with beans is reported to deter stem borers.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Once the caterpillars are inside the stem, sprays cannot reach them, so any treatment is only useful at the early whorl stage, before the caterpillars bore in. Ask the extension officer about timing.


## When to contact an extension officer
Contact an extension officer if many young plants show dead hearts, if you want advice on push-pull or resistant varieties, or if you cannot tell borer damage from fall armyworm damage.
