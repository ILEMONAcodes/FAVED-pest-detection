## Identification
Large cutworm: a fat, smooth-looking, greasy caterpillar that curls into a C shape when touched. The caterpillar hides in the soil by day and comes out at night. The model separates 'large' and 'yellow' cutworm, but the two are hard to tell apart in a photo, and the control advice is the same. Do not confuse with fall armyworm, which feeds on leaves and in the whorl rather than cutting seedlings at the base.


## Damage and symptoms
Young maize plants cut off at or just below soil level, or lying wilted at the base. Damage often appears in patches in the first weeks after the plants emerge. Sometimes plants are partly pulled into the soil. The caterpillars feed at night, so you rarely see them during the day.


## Conditions that favour it
Fields that were weedy before planting (moths lay eggs on weeds), fields with a lot of old crop residue, and young crops in the seedling stage. Cutworms are soil-dwelling, so damage is worst on young plants.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Clear weeds several weeks before planting so that young caterpillars have nothing to feed on. Till or hoe the soil before planting; this exposes caterpillars and pupae to birds and sun. In the morning, dig gently in the top few centimetres of soil around a freshly cut plant and kill the caterpillar you find. Replant gaps early. Rotate maize with a non-grass crop such as legumes.


## Biological and low-risk controls
Birds, ground beetles, and parasitic wasps feed on cutworms. Keep field edges and non-crop areas that shelter these helpers, and avoid spraying when the damage is small.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Because cutworms feed at night near the soil, treatments are normally aimed at the base of the plants in the late afternoon or evening; follow the label.


## When to contact an extension officer
Contact an extension officer if large patches of seedlings are being lost, if you are unsure whether the damage is from cutworms or another cause (for example termites or birds), or before you replant a large area.
