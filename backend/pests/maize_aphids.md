## Identification
Small soft-bodied insects, green to bluish-green or dark, in clusters on the leaves, in the whorl, and on the tassel. They are slow and do not jump. Some have wings. Do not confuse with dust or with the mould that follows aphid honeydew.


## Damage and symptoms
Curled or yellowing leaves, stunted plants when attacks are early and heavy, and a sticky 'honeydew' on leaves that later turns black with sooty mould. Heavy infestation on the tassel can interfere with pollination. Aphids can also carry virus diseases.


## Conditions that favour it
Dry spells and warm weather that favour fast aphid growth. Stressed plants from poor fertility or drought. Ants that protect aphids for their honeydew.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Keep the crop healthy with good spacing, fertility, and weed control. Check the whorl and tassel when scouting. Control ants if they are protecting colonies. A strong spray of plain water can knock small colonies off. Remove badly infested plants only if few are affected.


## Biological and low-risk controls
Ladybird beetles, hoverfly larvae, lacewings, and parasitic wasps often control aphids without any spray. Do not use broad-spectrum insecticides unless needed, because they kill these helpers and can make aphid problems worse.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Most maize crops do not need aphid sprays, because natural enemies usually bring populations down; check for helpful insects first.


## When to contact an extension officer
Contact an extension officer if aphids are heavy on young plants, if leaves show yellow streaks or mosaic patterns (possible virus), or before using any spray.
