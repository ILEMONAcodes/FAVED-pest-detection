## Identification
Common rust is a fungal leaf disease. It shows as small, round to oval, powdery pustules that are rusty brown to cinnamon-red, scattered on both sides of the leaf. Later in the season the pustules can turn blackish. Rubbing a pustule leaves orange-brown powder on your finger. Do not confuse with gray leaf spot, which gives long grey-tan rectangular lesions between the veins, or maize streak virus, which gives thin yellow streaks along the veins.


## Damage and symptoms
Many pustules on the leaves reduce the green leaf area, so the plant produces less grain. Heavy infection can dry the leaves early and reduce plant height. Losses are higher when infection starts early and is severe.


## Conditions that favour it
Cool, humid weather with dew or frequent light rain. Spores are carried by the wind, so rust can arrive from nearby fields. Susceptible varieties and late planting increase the risk.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Plant resistant or tolerant varieties; seed companies and IITA-linked programmes screen varieties for rust. Plant early, since late crops face more disease. Keep plants well fed and weeds under control. Because the spores blow in on the wind, rotation helps less than it does for diseases that stay on crop residue.


## Biological and low-risk controls
No biological product is widely recommended for maize rust. Resistant varieties are the main low-risk tool.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Fungicides are usually worth using only on a susceptible variety when the disease appears early and spreads quickly, such as on seed crops; for most smallholder maize, resistant varieties and early planting are more practical.


## When to contact an extension officer
Contact an extension officer for help choosing a resistant variety for your area, or if many leaves are covered with pustules before the plant flowers.
