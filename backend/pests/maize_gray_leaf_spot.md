## Identification
Gray leaf spot is a fungal disease. It makes long, narrow, rectangular grey to tan spots that run along the leaf veins on the upper leaf surface. Spots can join and turn the whole leaf brown. Small black dots may be seen on the underside. If you see thin yellow streaks along the veins instead, it is maize streak virus, not gray leaf spot.


## Damage and symptoms
Lower leaves are infected first, then the disease moves up the plant. Large dead areas reduce the leaf area that fills the grain. When infection comes early in the season, yield losses can be high.


## Conditions that favour it
The fungus survives on maize residue left on the soil surface from season to season, and spores are blown to lower leaves of the new crop in good weather. It is worse in fields with reduced tillage and continuous maize, and in warm, humid, cloudy conditions.


## Cultural and prevention practices
Rotate maize with legumes (beans, cowpeas, groundnuts) or with cereals other than maize. Plant resistant varieties; seed brochures may state resistance. Bury or clear residue by ploughing where this suits your soil, and clear maize debris before planting. Intercropping maize with beans reduced spore movement onto maize leaves and gave 21% higher yield in one trial, and combining tillage, intercropping and a resistant variety gave the best results.


## Biological and low-risk controls
There is no widely recommended biological product for gray leaf spot. Resistant varieties, rotation and residue management are the main low-risk tools.


## Chemical control (registered products only, label rates, protective equipment)
Spray only if scouting shows the problem is spreading. Use only products registered in Nigeria (NAFDAC) for this crop and pest, bought from a licensed dealer in the original labelled container. Follow the label for rate, timing, and the waiting period before harvest. Wear protective equipment (gloves, long sleeves, boots, mask, eye cover), never spray against the wind or near water, and keep children and animals away. Ask the extension officer which products are currently approved. Fungicides are an option on susceptible varieties when disease starts early and spreads, but cultural measures and resistant varieties should come first.


## When to contact an extension officer
Contact an extension officer to choose a resistant variety, to plan a rotation, or if lower leaves are covered with lesions before the plants flower.
